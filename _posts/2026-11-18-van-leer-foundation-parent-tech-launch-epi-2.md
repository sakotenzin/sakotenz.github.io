In a world full of tech launches, AI breakthroughs and promises of the next big thing, Van Leer Foundation came to us with a different kind of challenge: How could they bring more attention to child development in a new way? What could they do that they hadn’t done before?

The foundation works to improve the lives of babies, toddlers and the people who care for them. What they needed was greater awareness of that work, so we saw an opportunity to tap into a cultural moment to build relevance and raise awareness. Our mission became bringing Van Leer Foundation into a cultural conversation people were already paying attention to, giving audiences a fresh reason to care.

We’d all seen campaigns with babies at their heart. But we wanted to shift the perspective to the people raising them. For children to thrive, their parents need to thrive too. Supporting parents’ wellbeing is an essential part of supporting a child’s development. And with parents themselves watching, we wanted them to feel celebrated for the intelligence, creativity and resourcefulness they rarely give themselves credit for.

They manage households like CEOs, show the endurance of athletes and turn everyday moments into adventures. They sneak vegetables past suspicious little diners and decode requests made entirely in gibberish - all while navigating the practical and financial demands of raising a child.

Yet so much of this is taken for granted. We simply call it parenting.
That got us thinking about the capabilities we do stop to admire. Understanding context. Recognising patterns. Anticipating needs. The very things that make AI and smart assistants so impressive are things parents have been doing all along.
The contrast gave us a way to make familiar skills feel extraordinary again. If these abilities deserve headlines, launch events and applause when technology performs them, surely they deserve recognition when parents do.

And when it comes to the world’s toughest users, young children, even the smartest technology can struggle. A child’s request rarely arrives as a perfectly worded prompt. Understanding it takes familiarity, patience and a connection built through countless everyday interactions. Parents bring all of that.
When machines do it, we call it intelligence. When parents do it, we call it Tuesday.
So, during one of the biggest tech launch periods of the year, we helped Van Leer Foundation unveil the original smart assistant: parents.

The Parent Launch: OG Assistant borrows the language and conventions of smart-assistant marketing to give everyday parenting skills the launch treatment they deserve.
The humour comes from recognition. Parents know these moments intimately, but rarely hear them described with such admiration. Giving their everyday abilities the status of breakthrough features invites them, and everyone watching, to appreciate just how much goes into caring for a child.

Featuring the voices of Hala Hanna and Chris Duffy, the campaign ran across Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook and YouTube. A dedicated campaign page invited audiences to explore further, connecting the idea with facts and research about parents and young children.
By turning the excitement around artificial intelligence towards the intelligence of parents, we gave familiar acts of caregiving a fresh significance. That recognition also opened the door to the foundation’s wider message: being resourceful shouldn’t mean having to do it all alone.
If parents already do all this, imagine what they could do with your support.
